# BrandMenuCreator (BMC)

Use this brief in any image generator or AI assistant that accepts text instructions. Attach the approved Butter Jacket photo when the tool accepts a reference image. In the Jacket Club project, invoke the installed skill with `$brand-menu-create`.

Example request: “Apply BMC to the Chicken Tikka Jacket. Create a 4:3 menu image with the included crisp side salad and write website alt text.”

Start with the requested dish, its current menu entry and the intended use. The latest owner correction takes precedence. If the request is for a prompt only, return the reusable prompt and stop. For an image, inspect the [approved Butter Jacket reference](https://chatgpt.com/library?file_id=libfile_76ef485376c081919d66696542748af0&account_id=personal) and supply it to the image generator as the visual reference.

## The café’s visual signature

Stage the food on dark walnut with soft warm daylight from the upper left, gentle shadows and a seated diner’s three-quarter view. Use matte forest-green ceramic (#1F4D2B) for plated food. Keep the ingredient named in the title easy to identify at phone-thumbnail size. On the Butter Jacket the eye goes first to the pale potato and golden melting butter, then to the small red accents in the salad. For other dishes, find the equivalent focal ingredient in the actual recipe.

Use 4:3 landscape for the master menu image. Where square reuse is requested, keep the dish and plate inside the central 75% of the frame width with a little additional clearance. Inspect both crops. The reference establishes the lighting and colours; widen its tight framing when needed.

For designed menus, use cream (#F5E6C8) lettering on forest green, dark brown (#3B2416) on cream and butter gold (#F2B705) for small accents. Use the existing approved logo when branding a layout. Keep lettering, logo and price outside the food photograph.

## Apply the identity across the menu

- **Jackets:** Potato on the left, complimentary crisp side salad on the right. Every jacket includes it. Canonical range heading: “Every jacket comes with a free crisp side salad.” The Butter Jacket has crisp golden skin, fluffy flesh, one melting pat of butter and a lightly dressed salad of lettuce, cucumber, cherry tomatoes and red onion. Vary the filling to match each item.
- **Burgers, wraps, breakfast and sides:** Keep the surface, light and perspective. Turn the cut face or filling towards the customer. Show the actual portion, including the correct strip or nugget count. Add fries, dips or a drink only when included in the item shown.
- **Rice boxes and takeaway meals:** Use the actual serving container. Let the rice and topping read as separate elements, keeping the same background and light.
- **Coffee and cold drinks:** Use the correct cup or glass, with its rim and base in frame. Carry the palette through the setting. Keep foam, syrups and toppings faithful to the order.

## Copy for a Sunderland customer

Use plain British English, familiar dish names and prices from the current menu for the named sales channel. Put the dish name first, then the filling and what comes with it. Spell out optional extras and their price when provided. For counter boards, make the name and price readable at a glance; on mobile, keep the first line useful even when the description is collapsed. Use “Sunderland” or “Park Lane” where it helps someone identify or find the café. Local relevance comes from recognisable food and clear value. Claims such as local sourcing or bestseller status need evidence.

## Reusable image brief

“Create a photorealistic menu image of [dish] for The Jacket Club SR1 in Sunderland. Match the attached reference’s dark walnut table, warm upper-left daylight and three-quarter camera angle. Serve [actual portion] in [plate, cup or container]. Place [hero ingredient] where its texture is immediately visible. Include [confirmed sides and toppings]. Frame in 4:3 with [crop clearance for intended channel]. Keep the image free of lettering and extra dishes.”

For a series, reuse the approved reference directly for each generation to limit visual drift. Before delivery, compare the image with the recipe and reference, check portion and sides, and view it at thumbnail size. For website assets, supply descriptive alt text and small previews; reserve the large file for the enlarged view. Example alt text: “A steaming jacket potato with melting butter beside a dressed side salad on a forest-green plate.”
